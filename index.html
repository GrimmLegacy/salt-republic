The Drowned
Serenissima

The water reached the fourth step in 1494 and has risen a finger-width every year since. Venice did not drown all at once: it is still there, half under, still working, still keeping records of a year nobody admits to.

  • Four drowned realms, each with its own work

    A bell tower above the clouds, a flooded archive of century-old ledgers, a farm growing food eight hundred fathoms down, and a rail line running to the stars. Each quarter has its own people, its own jobs and its own price for doing them.

  • A deck of cards forged from your own story

    The jobs you take can hand you a card. The common ones are small favours. The rare ones arrive only because of who has come to trust you, and they carry real weight: they train your skills, open doors, and change how a quarter treats you. Carry four at a time and swap them freely.

  • The city keeps score of everything you do

    Everything you do is written down and kept. Land a job and it is struck off the list for good. The city opens its history one page at a time. Choose a faction and your story is shaped around it: every favour you win with one is counted against its rival.

  • The city keeps real hours

    Anno Domini 1530, counted day by day against your own clock. The season turns, the light changes, and some work only happens after dark, or only while the sun is still up. Come back tomorrow and the calendar has moved on without you.
